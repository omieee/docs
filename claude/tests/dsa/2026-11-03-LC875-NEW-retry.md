## Question as asked

The setup. Koko has four piles of bananas: [3, 6, 7, 11]. She has h = 8 hours. Each hour she picks one pile and eats k bananas from it. If the pile has fewer than k bananas left, she eats the whole pile and does nothing more in that hour. We want the smallest k that lets her finish all the piles within 8 hours.

Part (a): which way to move. Suppose your binary search tests speed k = 3. Koko needs 10 hours at this speed, which is more than 8. Which side of the range do you search next, the speeds below 3 or the speeds above 3? Why is it safe to throw away the other side completely?

Part (b): why binary search works here. There is no sorted list in this problem.
1. Which words in the problem statement tell you to think of binary search?
2. Write each speed from 1 to 11 as "finishes in time" or "does not finish in time". What pattern do those answers make, and why does that pattern make it safe to cut the range in half each round?

Part (c): a changed problem. A lift must carry three boxes, weighing [2, 8, 3], in this order and in at most 2 trips. A box cannot be split. We want the smallest lift capacity that does the job. You will search a range of capacities. What is the smallest capacity you would start the search from, and why that number and not 1?
