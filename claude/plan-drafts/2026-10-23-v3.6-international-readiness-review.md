## 1. Verdict

**If v3.6 is executed properly, Om is technically competitive for hands-on non-FAANG Senior roles in the Gulf, the Netherlands and Ireland, and at English-working employers in Germany. The curriculum is not India-specific. What is India-specific is the plan's market machinery, and what is missing is proof, not topics.**

Three things stand between v3.6 and an international offer, and more study hours fix none of them:

1. **The CV screen.** Recent production code is thin. Several postings screen on years of production work in one language. A personal project does not answer that.
2. **Live coding with strangers, in the formats European loops use.** Five of the six international loop reports have live coding with a human (the sixth, Aiven, is not detailed enough to tell). Outside Indian calibration loops, v3.6 has zero human coding mocks, no debugging-an-unfamiliar-codebase drill, and Om's current misses are explaining why and building from a blank page against a clock (section 2).
3. **The plan only reads the Indian market.** The harvest, the lane-size check, the extras triggers and resume v2 all run on Naukri searches, Bengaluru strings and a ₹ gate. International evidence never reaches them.

The fixes are small: about **3.5 hours of Om's time** in the core version, plus an optional 10-hour open-source contribution and a 3-hour conditional extra. Gate dates move by one week at most.

## 5. Question 3: the experience gap

**Credible where:** platform and backend-infrastructure roles. Om's current production record (IBM Cloud Databases on Kubernetes, the IKS upgrade programme at 300 clusters and 19k nodes, registry cost reduction) is real scale that a hiring manager can probe. PRCP, the Go agent and the controller then show recent code. Booking's "leading initiatives", "influence across all levels" and mentoring are requirements, so the tech-lead years are an asset there, not something to hide.

**Weak where:** product-backend roles that screen on one named language, sometimes with years (ai71 Python 8+, Tabby Go 2+, Bayut Python and Django, Core42 "Go expert"). Tabby's own posting softens its rule ("other stacks willing to learn Go" welcome), so these screens are softer than they read, but they still filter. By Feb 2028 Om will have about 16 months of part-time personal Go and no production Go unless the office supplies it.

**What overcomes the concern, strongest first:**

1. Production code at IBM that Om owns, recorded through the office proof path. Two production Go changes by Gate B turn "personal Go" into "production Go" on the CV. Zero study hours; it needs a conversation with his manager (F3).
2. Interview performance: live coding and a 30-minute deep dive that show fluency. This overrides the CV once Om is in the loop.
3. Public code a stranger can run and read: PRCP v1.0 with the controller, tests and CI (already in v3.6).
4. Optional: a reviewed upstream contribution to a known project, which is third-party evidence of his Go (F3).

**What a personal project cannot solve:** years-in-language screens; on-call and incident ownership as the primary owner of a service with real users; production scale numbers (PRCP's load test is synthetic); manager references for hands-on work. The answer to these is lane choice (platform roles, where IBM supplies the production story) and office work, not more study.

## 10. Question 8: final answers

- **Enough technically if executed perfectly?** For international non-FAANG Senior roles, yes, close to enough: 94% of required technical items are covered at least partly. For an offer, not by itself: three proofs are missing (section 1).
- **Top three risks to an international offer:**
  1. The CV screen: no recent production code that a foreign recruiter can see.
  2. Live coding with strangers: the current misses are explaining why and blank-page building, and 5 of the 6 international loop reports have live coding with a human.
  3. Targeting: applying to Java-first shops, German-required roles or language-years screens wastes the few high-value attempts. Underneath all three sits execution: 814 hours to Feb 2028, against a record that includes a 43-day gap.
- **Smallest changes with the most impact:** the office Go ask (0h); an international slice in the existing harvests (1h of Om's time); one human coding mock and two international calibration loops (1.5h plus campaign hours); two practice sessions turned into debug sessions (1h).
- **Evaluate at which gate?** Measure at Gate B, which is the first gate with the full technical bar (mocks with humans, reps, checklists, unseen set 2). Validate with two international calibration loops in 1c. Decide at Gate C, as section 11 already says. Gate A is too early: Go fundamentals only, 40 DSA, no design proof.
- **Non-FAANG vs FAANG international?** Yes, a real difference. FAANG companies run the same hiring process in Dublin as in the US (general knowledge, not re-verified in this review): two or more DSA rounds at a higher bar, design at scale, level-calibrated behaviour, no machine coding. v3.6's Gate C is roughly international non-FAANG readiness. FAANG needs Phase 2's own targets (DSA 150 / 110, 16 mocks).
- **Modify now or collect evidence first?** Both, split by cost. v3.6 is still a draft, so fold the zero-hour items into it before Om approves it (F1, F3 part a, F5's titles and lane, F2's wording bug). Hold the hour items (F2's sessions, F4, F3 part b) until the international slice of the November harvest closes (W7, Nov 22 2026). None of them is scheduled before W12, so waiting costs nothing.
