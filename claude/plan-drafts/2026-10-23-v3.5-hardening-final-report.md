ChatGPT's audit counted the same Go, Kubernetes, design and machine coding gaps. The Go gap in W42-W51 and the LLD gap are extra findings from this count.

**Why it happens.** A script fills the weeks by running each track's units in order and cutting them at week boundaries. It checks that the hours fit and that each unit comes after its dependencies. It does not check how long a track sits unused. In Kubernetes terms, the scheduler checks resource requests and ordering, but has no spread constraint. Tracks pile up early and then vanish. The same method leaves 118 "continued" pieces in the map, 26 of them 30 minutes or less, and each piece costs a restart.

### What the research says

These figures are taken from the papers' published summaries, which are linked in the sources.

- **Blocking is fine while you first learn a skill.** Interleaving (mixing topics) helps most when you must tell apart problem types that look alike. Brunmair and Richter's 2019 meta-analysis covered 59 studies and found an overall gain of g = 0.42, and a loss for some materials. So mix DSA patterns. Do not rotate unrelated tracks for the sake of mixing.
- **Skills you do not use decay.** Arthur and colleagues (1998, 53 studies) found that loss grows with time unused. Thinking-heavy, accuracy-scored skills lose more than physical or speed skills, and coding interviews are that type.
- **Space the reviews.** Cepeda and colleagues (2008, more than 1,350 people) found that the best gap between reviews grows with how long you must remember. For a test about a year away, the best gap was about 3 weeks.
- **Recall beats re-reading.** In Roediger and Karpicke (2006), people who tested themselves remembered 56% after a week. People who re-read remembered 42%.
- **The second language trips on "false friends."** In Shrestha and colleagues (2020), developers learning another language hit wrong assumptions carried over from the old one. For Go after Python: nil vs None, slice sharing vs list copying, error values vs exceptions, goroutines vs asyncio.

### What your week looks like under C

- **Every study day at 10:30:** DSA first, at most 45 minutes (unchanged). Then the main lane, one unit at a time where possible.
- **One fixed day a week (you pick it):** DSA, then a **40-minute retrieval rep** for the idle track that has waited longest, then the week's design or machine coding piece.
- **Bedside:** watch items first (unchanged), then 10 minutes of question cards from closed units. These are reading only and need no audio.
- **Sunday:** the weekly close (unchanged).

A **retrieval rep** means redoing a small task from a closed unit, from a blank file or a fresh kind cluster, with no notes and no AI, and then checking it against asserts or `kubectl` output. It works like a DSA cold re-solve, applied to other tracks. Examples:

- Go: rewrite the G2 slice-sharing test from memory and add one new case.
- Kubernetes: break one scenario from K7 on kind and fix it.
- Design: redraw one building block with its failure mode.

A rep gets no score, only "held" or "slipped", and one line saying what slipped.

This keeps your rule that short ad hoc slots do not work (Oct 14). The rep sits inside the fixed morning block, on a fixed day. ChatGPT proposed two 25-minute Go contacts a week instead. I did not take that: short boxes have overrun before (the LC141 cold box ran about 2 hours against 15 minutes on Oct 6), and two contacts double the restarts.
